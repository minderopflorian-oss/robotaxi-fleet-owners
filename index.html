How to fund your fleet
with Michiel van Duin.

Tesla to Mars #39 - FSD is a $10K feature to launch a $10B business. We CRACKED the code to FUND it!

Former MisterGreen executive Michiel van Duin joins the Tesla to Mars hosts on financing Teslas, leasing FSD and how investors and communities could co-own autonomous fleets.

Watch episode #39YouTube channel

Cash for car 1

Simplest start: no lender, no interest, full control while the rules are still unclear.

Personal or dealer loan

Familiar route for a first car. Check whether the lender allows commercial or robotaxi use.

Private or operational lease

Lease the car instead of owning it. Mister Green (NL) has leased thousands of Teslas, including FSD, and has a robotaxi page at mistergreen.nl/robotaxi. Check that ride-hailing use is allowed in the contract.

Business loan via a BV or LLC

Put the fleet in a company. Cleaner books, possible tax benefits and easier to add cars, but more admin.

Equipment or asset finance

The car itself is the collateral, as with taxis and trucks. Common once you run more than one car.

Co-ownership with an agreement

Share a car with friends or family. Put ownership, costs, payouts and exit rules in writing first.

Start with one car, reinvest into car 2

Use real earnings from car 1 to fund the next. Slower, but based on your own numbers, not estimates.

Check local EV incentives

Subsidies, tax schemes and lower road tax differ per country and city. Look them up before you choose a route.

Unknowns lenders will ask about

  • The revenue split per mile between Tesla and the owner (not announced)
  • Expected paid hours per day in your city
  • Insurance cost and who is liable
  • Depreciation and resale value of a robotaxi
  • Whether your country and city allow owner-run robotaxis, and when

What lenders want to see

  • ☐ A simple business plan: city, number of cars, timeline
  • ☐ Cost estimate per car: purchase, charging, insurance, cleaning, parking
  • ☐ Revenue scenarios: low, base and high utilisation
  • ☐ Your own contribution (down payment)
  • ☐ Company structure (BV, LLC or private)
  • ☐ Insurance quote or plan
  • ☐ Charging and parking setup
  • ☐ Plan B: what the car does if robotaxi income is late

An idea in development

The CyberFund.

Co-own a robotaxi fleet without running it yourself. The CyberFund is a pooled fund that would buy and operate robotaxis, with fleet operators ("shepherds") running the cars day to day.

POOL

A shared fund

Many people put money into one fund instead of each buying and running their own car.

BUY

The fund buys robotaxis

The fund owns a fleet, so you co-own part of it without running a car yourself.

OPERATE

Shepherds run the cars

Local fleet operators, called shepherds, handle charging, cleaning and keeping the cars on the road.

SHARE

Co-own the future

Cities, investors and communities could co-own autonomous fleets together.

Why a fund?

Robotaxis could flip how car value works: instead of only losing value, a car that earns money while it drives can stay productive for years. A fund lets more people take part, and spreads the work across experienced operators.

Where does the idea come from?

From Tesla to Mars episode #39, a deep dive with Michiel, a former MisterGreen executive and financial architect behind early FSD funding strategies. It covers how MisterGreen financed 5,000+ Teslas with FSD, the economics of leasing FSD, and a new class of investors and shepherds.

Is it available?

Not yet. Our team is still developing the idea. There are no returns, fees, minimums or launch dates yet.

Semi as a Service · a concept

Pay per mile.
Not per truck.

Our idea: offer the Tesla Semi to hauliers on a pay-per-mile basis, so smaller European transport companies can go electric without a big upfront truck purchase, and get ready for autonomous trucking.

This is a concept being explored by an independent community. It is not a Tesla offer, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by Tesla, Inc.

HOW IT WORKS

Pay per mile vs buying

Instead of buying a truck and running it yourself, you pay per mile driven. The price could cover the truck, charging and maintenance, with insurance as an option.

WHO IT'S FOR

Smaller hauliers

European transport companies with a few to a few dozen trucks, for whom the upfront cost of an electric truck is the biggest hurdle.

WHY NOW

Semi + autonomy + EU rules

The Semi is in production and heading to Europe, autonomy is on Tesla's roadmap, and EU rules on emissions and automated driving keep moving.

Energy as a Service · a concept

Charge the fleet.
Skip the capex.

The idea: Cybercab and Semi fleet owners get depot charging plus solar and battery storage (for example Tesla Megapack or Powerwall, with chargers) without the upfront investment, paying a monthly fee or per kWh.

This is a concept being explored by an independent community. It is not a Tesla offer, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by Tesla, Inc.

Lower cost per km

Own solar and stored energy can cut what each km costs to charge.

Grid congestion workaround

A battery buffers the depot so a small grid connection can still charge a fleet. Very relevant in the Netherlands.

Smart charging

Charge at the cheapest hours, from the grid or from storage.

Possible extra revenue

The battery could earn from grid services or energy trading when the cars don't need it.

Who it's for

FLEETS

Small robotaxi fleets

Owners planning a few Cybercabs who need a place to charge.

TRUCKS

Hauliers

Transport companies moving to electric trucks like the Semi.

SITES

Depots and parking

Owners of depots and parking sites with space for chargers, solar and storage.

When can private owners add cars to the network?

On Tesla's Q3 2026 earnings call, Tesla said customer cars should start joining the robotaxi fleet around Q2 2027.

When can I buy a Cybercab?

Tesla said Cybercab deliveries to customers start by the end of 2026. Elon Musk also said the vast majority of Cybercabs will be Tesla-owned, so expect small private numbers at first.
